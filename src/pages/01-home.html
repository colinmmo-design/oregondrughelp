Free help anywhere in Oregon

You don’t have to figure this out alone.

Clear, kind information about drugs, alcohol, treatment and recovery in Oregon, for you or for someone you love. No judgment, no jargon.

Talk to someone today Find where to start

Need help with drugs or alcohol in Oregon? Start here.

  • Talk to someone now: call the Oregon Alcohol & Drug Helpline at 1-800-923-4357. It’s free, confidential and open 24/7.
  • Pay nothing for treatment: the Oregon Health Plan (OHP) covers detox, medication, outpatient and residential treatment at no cost to members. How to get free treatment
  • Overdose: call 911 and give naloxone (Narcan). Oregon law protects people who call for help from drug possession charges. What to do
  • Local help: every Oregon county has a Behavioral Health Resource Network with screening, peer support and treatment. Find yours

What help can look like

There’s no single route. Most people use a mix of these over time.

Medical detox
Supervised care to get through withdrawal safely. Withdrawal from alcohol and some drugs can be dangerous on your own.
Medication
Prescribed treatments like buprenorphine and methadone that ease cravings and withdrawal for opioids, plus options for alcohol.
Therapy and counseling
One-on-one or group sessions that build skills for cravings, stress and relationships.
Outpatient care
Treatment that fits around work, school and family, with regular visits.
Residential programs
Stay somewhere safe and structured for a few weeks or months while you focus on recovery.
Peer support
Peer mentors and groups led by people who’ve been there, in person or online.

When it’s someone you love

You can’t make someone change, but how you show up makes a real difference.

Start with care, not blame

Pick a calm, sober moment. Say what you’ve seen and how you feel: “I’ve noticed you seem exhausted, and I’m worried about you.”

Keep them safer

Keep naloxone at home and learn how to use it. Ask them not to use alone. Staying alive comes first.

Look after yourself

Family support groups, counseling and helplines are there for you too. Call 1-800-923-4357 even if your loved one isn’t ready.
