Is drug possession illegal in Oregon?

Yes. Oregon decriminalized small amounts of drugs in 2021, then made possession a crime again in 2024. Here’s what the law says now and what it means if you’re looking for help.

Last reviewed November 3, 2026 · Sources listed at the bottom of this page

The short answer

  • Yes, it’s illegal. Since September 1, 2024, possessing small amounts of drugs for personal use is a misdemeanor in Oregon under House Bill 4002.
  • Possible penalties include up to 180 days in jail and up to 18 months of probation.
  • Deflection programs in some counties steer people to treatment instead of prosecution. Counties choose whether to offer them.
  • Treatment funding stayed. HB 4002 kept the treatment side of Measure 110, including local Behavioral Health Resource Networks.
  • Overdose calls are still protected. Oregon’s Good Samaritan law still shields people who call 911 during an overdose from possession charges.

How Oregon’s drug law changed

WhenWhat happened
November 2020Voters pass Measure 110, making possession of small amounts of drugs a civil violation, like a traffic ticket, and funding treatment.
2021Senate Bill 755 sets up Behavioral Health Resource Networks in every county.
April 2024Governor Kotek signs House Bill 4002, making possession a crime again.
September 1, 2024Possession becomes a misdemeanor. Counties may start deflection programs.

What this means if you’re looking for help

  • Asking for treatment is not a crime. Calling a helpline, seeing a doctor or starting treatment won’t get you arrested.
  • Treatment is confidential. Federal privacy rules strictly protect substance use treatment records.
  • Always call 911 in an overdose. The Good Samaritan law still protects you from possession charges.
  • If you’ve been charged, ask your lawyer whether your county offers deflection or a treatment option, which may keep the charge off your record.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws change; talk to a lawyer about your own situation.

Common questions

Is drug possession a crime in Oregon in 2026?

Yes. Since September 1, 2024, possessing a small amount of a controlled substance for personal use is a misdemeanor in Oregon, under House Bill 4002.

What happened to Measure 110?

Measure 110, passed by voters in 2020, made possession of small amounts of drugs a non-criminal violation starting in 2021 and put cannabis tax money toward treatment. In 2024 the Legislature passed HB 4002, which made possession a crime again but kept much of Measure 110’s treatment funding and services.

What is the penalty for drug possession in Oregon?

Under HB 4002, possession is a misdemeanor that can bring up to 180 days in jail. A person convicted can be placed on probation for up to 18 months.

What is a deflection program in Oregon?

Deflection programs let police refer people found with drugs to treatment and services instead of filing charges. Each county decides whether to run one, so availability varies across Oregon.

Can drug possession records be cleared in Oregon?

HB 4002 allows some records to be sealed or expunged. Rules depend on the case, so talk to a lawyer or legal aid organization about your situation.
