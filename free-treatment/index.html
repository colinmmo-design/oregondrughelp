Free and low-cost drug and alcohol treatment in Oregon

How to get treatment paid for through the Oregon Health Plan, private insurance or Medicare, and where to turn if you have no insurance at all.

Last reviewed November 3, 2026 · Sources listed at the bottom of this page

The short answer

  • On the Oregon Health Plan (OHP), treatment is free. OHP covers detox, medication, counseling, outpatient and residential treatment at no cost to members.
  • Apply for OHP any time at 800-699-9075 (Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.) or at oregonhealthplan.gov.
  • No referral needed: OHP members can usually start substance use treatment without seeing their regular doctor first.
  • No insurance? Call 1-800-923-4357 for free referrals to programs that serve uninsured people, and ask about sliding-scale fees.

The Oregon Health Plan pays for treatment

The Oregon Health Plan (OHP) is Oregon’s Medicaid program. If you’re a member, drug and alcohol treatment costs you nothing. OHP covers:

  • Withdrawal management (detox), with medical supervision
  • Medication for opioid use disorder, including methadone and buprenorphine (Suboxone)
  • Counseling and therapy, one-on-one or in groups
  • Outpatient and intensive outpatient programs
  • Residential treatment, where you live at the program for a time

You can usually start substance use treatment without a referral from your doctor. Some services, like residential treatment, may need approval from your OHP plan first. The program you choose can usually handle that for you.

Already on OHP? Call the member services number on your OHP card and say you’re looking for substance use treatment. They’ll help you find a provider in your plan.

If you have private insurance or Medicare

Most private health plans and Medicare cover addiction treatment. Call the number on your insurance card and ask about your substance use disorder benefits: which programs are in network, what you’ll pay, and whether you need approval first.

If you have no insurance

  1. Apply for OHP. Many people who think they won’t qualify do, especially if their income has dropped.
  2. Call for a referral. The Oregon Alcohol & Drug Helpline (1-800-923-4357) and SAMHSA’s National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) can point you to programs that serve uninsured people, and both are free.
  3. Contact your county’s Behavioral Health Resource Network. Every Oregon county has one. They bill insurance when you have it, and can connect you to services when you don’t. Find yours.
  4. Ask about sliding-scale fees. Many treatment programs lower their prices based on income. FindTreatment.gov lets you search for them.

Common questions

Is drug rehab free in Oregon?

For Oregon Health Plan (OHP) members, yes. OHP covers substance use treatment at no cost, including detox, medication, outpatient care and residential treatment. Without OHP, cost depends on your insurance, but many programs offer sliding-scale fees.

Does the Oregon Health Plan cover rehab?

Yes. OHP covers withdrawal management (detox), medication such as methadone and buprenorphine, counseling, outpatient programs and residential treatment. Some services, especially residential care, may need approval from your OHP plan first.

Do I need a referral to get treatment on OHP?

Usually not. OHP members can generally start mental health or substance use treatment without a referral from their primary care provider. Call the member services number on your OHP card to find a provider.

How do I apply for the Oregon Health Plan?

Call 800-699-9075 (Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific) or apply online through oregonhealthplan.gov. You can apply at any time of year, and local community partners can help you fill out the application for free.

Can I get drug treatment in Oregon without insurance?

Yes. Apply for OHP first, since many people qualify. Meanwhile, call the Oregon Alcohol & Drug Helpline at 1-800-923-4357 or SAMHSA at 1-800-662-4357 for referrals to programs that serve uninsured people, and contact your county’s Behavioral Health Resource Network.
